Who this guide is for

If you’re a taller rider (around 6′ and up) or a heavier rider (around 300 lbs and up) — or both — most e-bike buying guides aren’t written for you. Standard advice assumes an average-size rider. The specs that matter most for you are different: published payload capacity, real rider-height fit, torque for getting moving, and brakes and tires rated for the load.

How we researched this

We have not ridden, lab-tested, or personally owned any of these bikes, and we don’t claim otherwise. Every specification below comes from the manufacturer’s own published specifications, and each bike links to its source product page so you can verify anything yourself. Where a manufacturer doesn’t publish a spec, we say so rather than guessing. Range figures are the manufacturer’s claimed range — typically measured with a light test rider under ideal conditions — not independently measured real-world range.

A note on prices: “True cut” means the bike’s own price was actually reduced. “Bundle value” means the discount figure includes free accessories at the retailer’s own valuation — the bike itself didn’t get cheaper. We distinguish the two everywhere.

The bikes, in detail

Mokwheel Tor Plus — highest payload per dollar

450-lb payload is the highest we’ve seen at this price, and the 5′6″–6′8″ height range is the tallest reach in this guide. Full geometry is published (29″ standover, 17″ reach, 46″ wheelbase). The 90Nm+ hub motor and 720Wh battery are solid for the money. At $1,199.99 after a verified $200 price cut, it’s the value pick for heavy riders.
Limitations: step-over frame only; 81 lbs is heavy to maneuver; range test conditions aren’t published. Source: mokwheel.com

Mokwheel Basalt ST 2.0 — the 450-lb step-through

Same 450-lb payload as the Tor Plus but with both step-through and step-over frames — the only 450-lb step-through we’ve found — and the biggest battery here at 940Wh with a claimed 80-mile range. Two sizes cover 5′2″ to 6′8″. Currently $1,799.99 after a verified $200 cut.
Limitations: 80 lbs; we couldn’t confirm how long the $1,999.99 reference price was the actual selling price, so treat the “was” price with appropriate caution. Source: mokwheel.com

Velotric Nomad 2 — the payload king (505 lbs)

No other bike here touches the 505-lb published payload (plus 1,000-lb towing). If you’re near 400 lbs and carry cargo, this is the headroom pick. 90Nm motor, 705.6Wh UL-certified battery, and Velotric’s 5-year frame warranty is the longest frame coverage in this guide.
Limitations: no actual price cut — the “$549.99 savings” is a free spare battery, not a cheaper bike ($1,999 either way). Claimed 65-mile range was measured with a 187-lb rider; expect less at higher rider weights. Source: velotricbike.com

Velotric Discover 2 — the deal right now

$500 off ($1,999 → $1,499) is the deepest verified true price cut we’ve found on any heavy-duty e-bike. 440-lb payload, 63 lbs (lightest full-size option here), and a comfortable step-through commuter design. The 75Nm motor is the mildest torque in this group — fine on flats, less eager on steep hills at full load.
Limitations: step-through only; lowest torque here; claimed range figures assume a 187-lb rider. Source: velotricbike.com

Velotric Summit 1 — the tall rider’s trail option

The Large frame fits riders up to 6′6″ — the tallest step-over fit here — with 440-lb payload, 90Nm motor, and proper 27.5×2.6″ trail tires. Currently $1,599 after a $400 true cut (the discount deepened this week, so re-verify the price).
Limitations: step-over only; 62 lbs; sale pricing is moving — verify before buying. Source: velotricbike.com

Aventon Aventure 3 — the known quantity

400-lb payload, fat 26×4.0″ tires, 733Wh LG-cell battery, and both step-over and step-through frames. Aventon’s warranty is a standout: 2 years on the frame, extended to lifetime if you register within 90 days. No discount right now ($1,999), but pricing is stable.
Limitations: no current discount; Aventon doesn’t publish standover height; 76–77 lbs. Source: aventon.com

Aventon Aventure M — maximum torque

The 100Nm mid-drive motor is the highest torque figure published by any brand here, and mid-drives climb more efficiently than hub motors at heavy loads. 400-lb payload, 733Wh battery, claimed 85-mile range (the highest claim here — still a manufacturer claim).
Limitations: $2,899 is the priciest bike here; step-over only; no discount; Aventon doesn’t publish standover. Source: aventon.com

Ride1Up Vorsa — strongest hub motor, lightest chassis

95Nm is the highest hub-motor torque in this guide, on a 65-lb chassis (lightest full-size frame here). 440-lb payload, full published geometry, and three wheel/tire configurations including a fat-tire option. From $1,495.
Limitations: 720Wh battery is our computation (48V×15Ah), not a manufacturer-stated figure; range conditions are vaguely specified (“30–60 mi”); 1-year warranty is the thinnest coverage here; no discount. Note: Ride1Up’s own pages contain small typos in height ranges — we quote them verbatim and footnote rather than correct. Source: ride1up.com

Himiway D7 — the heavyweight bruiser

400-lb payload, a massive 960Wh battery, and a 1000W motor — the most raw capacity here. Currently $2,499 after a verified $500 cut.
Limitations: 88.6 lbs — you’ll feel every pound maneuvering it; Himiway doesn’t publish torque for this motor or text-readable geometry (spec tables are images); the brand’s own page conflicts on tire size (4.8″ vs 4.5″); step-over only. Source: himiwaybike.com

Our analysis: how to choose

The following is our analysis based on the manufacturer specifications above — not manufacturer claims.

  • If you’re around 400 lbs: restrict yourself to 450–505 lb payload bikes (Tor Plus, Basalt ST 2.0, Nomad 2, D7/D7 Pro). At exactly 400 lbs on a 400-lb-rated bike you have zero margin for cargo, locks, or groceries — the 450-lb bikes give you real headroom.
  • If you’re 350–380 lbs: any 400+ lb bike works; prioritize torque (Aventure M, Vorsa, D7) if your routes include hills.
  • If you’re 6′4″ or taller: check the Large/XL height ceiling first — Summit 1 (6′6″), Tor Plus and Basalt ST 2.0 (6′8″) fit tallest.
  • If you want a step-through: your 400+ lb options are Basalt ST 2.0, Discover 2, Aventure 3 Step-Through, and Vorsa ST.
  • Best value right now: Discover 2 at $1,499 (−$500 verified cut) or Tor Plus at $1,199.99 (−$200 verified cut) — the two genuine discounts with the most bike per dollar.
