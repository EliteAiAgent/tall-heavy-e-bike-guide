Best E-Bikes for Riders Around 300–400 lb

Published: October 6, 2026 · Prices verified: October 6, 2026 (re-check before buying)

The one rule

Your weight plus everything you carry must stay under the bike’s published maximum payload. That means: your body weight + backpack + lock + groceries + child seat + any cargo ≤ the payload number the manufacturer publishes.

A 380-lb rider with a 15-lb backpack and a 10-lb lock is a 405-lb load. That rider needs a bike rated above 405 lbs — not a bike rated “about 400.” We apply this rule strictly below: we never recommend a bike for a rider whose loaded weight would exceed the manufacturer’s published payload specification.

If you ride around 400 lbs

Only bikes with a 400-lb or higher published payload qualify, and at exactly 400 lbs of body weight, a 400-lb-rated bike leaves zero margin for cargo. If you carry anything, you want 450+.

Bikes rated at 330 lbs become eligible — but read the margin warning. A 330-lb rider on a 330-lb-rated bike has zero margin for a lock, let alone groceries. We’d still prefer 350+ lb bikes; the 330-lb options are for riders who travel light and want the lower price.

What about e-trikes?

The Lectric XP Trike2 advertises 415 lbs of total capacity — but the rider weight limit is 330 lbs (the rest is basket capacity). Trikes are a different vehicle class with different handling. We’re not recommending trikes for 300–400 lb riders based on the total-capacity number, because the rider limit is what matters.

Bikes we excluded, and why

  • Ride1Up Prodigy V2 — 300-lb published payload cap. A 300-lb rider with any cargo exceeds it. Excluded per our rule.
  • Aventon Sinch 2.5 — discontinued and out of stock; not purchasable.
  • Any bike without a published payload — if the manufacturer won’t publish the number, we can’t verify it fits you, so it’s out.
